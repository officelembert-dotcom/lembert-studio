There are infinite games one can play, but usually we dont. We get so used to the games we play now that we forget what it was like to be a kid, with the freedom to invent games, play games, and stop to play games that no longer were fun. We'd end up joining games already played by everyone, which offer little joy and freedom, but at least we aren't alone in them.&#x20;

**Person 1:** “I have done everything… I traveled the world, I built my business, I moved countries, I went to parties, I feel like I did it all.” “I don’t know what else to want.”

**Person 2:** “I would love to grow the business, but I can’t work anymore. I am already full; my days are full, and to work anymore I would need to not sleep.”

Person 3: "I always played this game. I dont know any other game. What if I fail in another game? Let me rather keep playing the current game."

**Person 4:** “I am good, things are ok, I just need to sustain what is, and that’s fine, why stretch myself? Why challenge myself? As long as I get by, it’s ok.”

Sure it is. There is no one forcing you to a new game. You can keep playing the old one. The game of getting by, linear growth, not taking a risk, looking not too foolish in the eyes of others, and satisfying the expectations you believe are upon you. Or you can conclude life, saying what you now know is what there is to know, that you have learned the game, that you have seen the magic available to you, this is all that there is.

There is no problem with that game. Most people play that game. I found myself playing that game many times. Just don’t stretch too much; something bad could happen.

It’s the game of trying to make the box in which you swim comfortable enough so we don’t have to step out of it.

Again there is no problem with that. It’s not that at the end of your life there is gonna be someone coming saying, “Wow, you played a really small game; you should have played full out, challenged your assumptions and truly gone all in on the craziest, biggest, and most outrageous idea of what your life could be, what would be the utmost expression of your soul. You wasted your life!”

Yes, you could judge yourself, but what for? What difference would it make by the end of your life? None. So if that’s the life you live, and want to live, enjoy it. Be in the box and make it comfortable, and don’t rock the boat.

But if you read this far, and you are still reading, you get this little tingling, this feeling of “ok come on challenge me, get me out of my rut, show me the outside of my box!!!” Then maybe you are cut for a different game. A game that doesn’t ask how well am I playing, but rather asks how much life am I expressing, what do I dare to imagine, and if I knew I couldn’t fail, what would I play for?

**Idea 1: In life there is absolutely nothing serious going on**

Before I explain, let me tell you that ideas are not truth, they are simply ideas, which we can try on and see what they do to us.

See, most people live by the assumption that life is a serious business, and that there are things to lose, that you and I need to be careful to not mess it up, and that we need to hold on to things because we know one day we lose it all.

Now that’s one way to live, one way to play the game, and again you are free to join 99.9% of humanity playing from that assumption.

I just don’t think it’s much fun. What if instead I played from the idea that there is absolutely nothing serious going on here, that there is nothing to lose, as truly there is nothing. It’s a game; the game has consequences, stakes, and certain games have certain rules, but the thought of death could also free you to see that the game is set up from the beginning in such a way that there is nothing to lose, as you never owned anything to begin with. It just depends on how you look at the symbol of death.

Imagine going through life having let go of the idea of loss, and instead you saw everything as an expression of the one formless energy science today calls the Higgs field, and humans have called god for the most part. Imagine taking the perspective that this all-pervasive energy has now taken form as you living your life, having your opportunities and challenges, and would realize the absolute wonder of being you for this brief little moment in time (have a listen to Alan Watts at this point: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wU0PYcCsL6o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wU0PYcCsL6o)).

Now again, it’s an idea, not the truth, and all we have done is play with 1 of millions of ideas of what just being you could mean and the game available to you.

Yet how small have your ideas of life become? Do they have to stay that way? And what would become available if you would take on another idea?

**Idea 2: Nothing needs to happen before you can choose a different game**

Most people live in an idea of time and causation that only allows for things to change when the imagined conditions are met. Like before you can choose a new game, you first have to be wise, courageous, smart, big or good enough. Or you need proof that you are not making a fool of yourself and choosing the wrong game. Or there needs to be some certainty and testing of ideas before you can choose the new game. Or you need to become a new person before you can choose the new game.

But what if that’s just another rule you invented in the current game? What if the new game didn’t have that rule. In the new game you could simply choose to say, “This is my game, I’ll be growing this business to 20,000,000 while working less and having more fun.”

The old game would say, “Based on what can you say that?” But in the new game you didn’t need to base it on anything. Nothing needed to happen before you could declare a new game as you created it by declaring it. No need to wait.

**Idea 3: What if nothing needs time?**

“Dude, I know this takes time!”

But what if not? What if time was never the condition? Doesn’t make any sense? Good!

Because it doesn’t make any sense to the linear mind. The linear mind believes there is a certain progression, and it has already concluded how quickly or slowly things tend to go. When you think about creating anything, you think in time. How much time will this take, and this take and this take, and that means the whole thing will take so long. And then you go and act on it.

But what if things only ever happened now. Which they do. And your idea of time was completely wrong. That things could actually happen suddenly, could be uncaused, just appearing out of thin air… Ok, I lost you here. I am not saying that things happen uncaused; maybe they do, but here is what this idea intends to do: it pokes into your model of time. See, you have made conclusions about how much time your dreams will take, how much time you have left, how much time you have already passed and how much time you have in a day. And in that model of time and progression, all your reasoning for why things will take long is valid; the only thing is that your model is wrong.

Over and over again did I have the experience of helping people collapse the time they thought something would take. I remember having a client in one of my seminars who wanted to create the money for his wedding. At first it seemed impossible to him, as he was thinking based on how long he usually took to create the money it would take. Then we played an impossible game; he then declared he would create it within the week of the seminar, and in the end it took him 1 phone call on that day. He collapsed time from months to a week to within the hour, and all of that happened now.

What if nothing needs time?

So here we are; we just spent some minutes together, and there are 3 possibilities I see for where you are at.

1. Nice read, let me get back to my day, and hopefully I’ll take some inspiration from reading this and somehow my game will become more exciting and greater. Would be nice.
2. Makes no sense, some esoteric bs, easy to write but has no utility.
3. I know somewhere that there is truth here; something is speaking to me. I can’t justify it, but it is there.

If you are in camp 1, enjoy; maybe the ideas will percolate, the game will emerge, and you’ll come back to read again, or find other sources of inspiration.

If you are in camp 2, totally understand. Enjoy your view and I wish you well.

If you are in camp 3 and you got tickled by this little exploration and you know there is a game waiting for you, reach out. I’ll be sending you a list of questions that will help you reveal more of that game, and if you are serious (but not serious) about playing a new game, and it fits my game, let us tango.

Enjoy, life is magical,

Moritz
