The difference between leadership and management: Many confuse the functions of leadership and management, thinking they mean the same thing. Many with the title of leadership are in reality, managers, whereas some managers are in reality, leaders. The core difference between the two is that a manager deals with the reported world. They deal with what their dashboard is showing them: what people are doing, how we could do it better, and the current state of how things are. Leadership, although taking into account the current state of how things are, functions in the domain of the future, and powerful leadership functions in the domain of the created future. The created future is not limited by or tied to the reported and current state of affairs. The created future is something the leader calls forth by saying, "This is where we're going. This is what we're doing. This is what we are creating," and that "this" they're referring to can be as large or small as the leader is willing to stand for. Look at some examples of this:

* When John F. Kennedy decided, "We choose to go to the Moon," that wasn't what was current or reported, nor was it what most people at the time would deem possible.
* "I want for India complete independence in the full English sense of that English term," when spoken by Gandhi, was not what the reports would tell you is happening.
* Your company growing 10x within the next 5 years is not what's in the books. Now, things like that aren't in the reports. They come from people who create those possibilities by speaking them and being willing to stand behind them.

There's something magical that happens when you speak a new reality into being, without having figured it out, without knowing how, but with the will to say, "This is where we are going." Great management will for sure be necessary, but great management doesn't create the future. Yet every great manager can lead the creation of something that otherwise wouldn't exist. It's not in the reports; it's not in the current. That is in the unspoken future.
